## Design Context

### Users

Open Signal serves a layered public audience. The front page must be legible to informed generalists who want to understand consequential public changes quickly, while the evidence and Claim layers must withstand verification by policy, industry, and research professionals.

The core reading modes are:

- about 30 seconds to identify the most important current signal;
- about 2 minutes to scan the distribution of meaningful changes;
- about 5 minutes to understand one judgment, its evidence, and its uncertainty;
- deeper investigation through the Evidence overlay and permanent Claim page.

The product is public, read-only, non-personalized, and is not an investment, legal, or automated decision tool.

### Brand Personality

The brand should feel restrained, curious, and traceable. It should create calm curiosity and justified trust rather than urgency, spectacle, or borrowed institutional authority.

Open Signal may make explicit editorial judgments, but it earns trust by distinguishing Observation, Analysis, and Open Signal Assessment; showing uncertainty and counterevidence; abstaining when evidence is inadequate; and preserving what it previously published.

### Aesthetic Direction

The interface is a dark editorial instrument, not a literal trading terminal, fixed dashboard, cyberpunk control room, or generic AI news product.

- Narrative content uses highly readable editorial typography; monospaced typography is reserved for numbers, timestamps, identifiers, state transitions, and provenance.
- Color is semantic and supplemental. Direction, status, Section identity, maturity, and confidence must not be conflated or communicated by color alone.
- The stable site shell and reading rhythm frame dynamically selected Sections and domain-native Components.
- Desktop supports scanning plus evidence verification. Mobile uses a deliberately reordered, glance-first flow with full-screen evidence sheets rather than merely shrinking the desktop layout.
- English is the launch language, but layout, content models, typography, and UI architecture must remain locale-ready for Chinese, Japanese, French, RTL scripts, and other future languages.
- The Public Beta quality bar includes the full seven-Slot publication surface, all eight Component Families, responsive variants, and the Evidence/Claim overlay, including the backend work needed to support truthful Render Plans.

### Design Principles

1. **A living front page, not a daily reset.** The current page is periodically recomposed as verified Section output changes. Older material may remain while it is semantically valid, with honest timestamps and a registry-driven retirement policy. A Section change or hard expiry triggers a full-page compile; only a complete, verified, immutable snapshot may atomically replace the current front page. If compilation or verification fails, the last verified snapshot remains public and its freshness state stays explicit.
2. **Evidence before decoration.** Every visual encoding must correspond to real data or a defined relationship. Missing evidence always degrades toward a simpler, more conservative representation.
3. **Editorial hierarchy over card uniformity.** Use a fixed page skeleton, dynamic Section selection, adaptive Component Families, and meaningful variation in Lead, Secondary, Feed, Main, Utility, and Archive regions.
4. **Sparse can be trustworthy.** Never lower evidence standards or manufacture content to fill space. When active material is limited, use intentional space, Archive comparisons, Method, and System State.
5. **Progressive disclosure preserves both clarity and accountability.** The front page shows a compact trust layer; the overlay exposes evidence, counterevidence, methods, and lineage; the permanent Claim page preserves the complete public record.
6. **Freshness must be semantically precise.** Distinguish source observation time, Claim publication or verification time, page composition time, and any later material update. A generic “updated” label must never blur these meanings.
7. **Internationalization and accessibility are structural constraints.** Avoid fixed English-length assumptions, use logical layout properties, preserve meaningful hierarchy at text zoom, respect reduced motion, and target WCAG AA or better.
